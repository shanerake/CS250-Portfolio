# CS 250 Software Development Lifecycle
**Shane Rake**

This repository holds my Sprint Review and Retrospective from CS 250. The project was built around SNHU Travel, a travel booking app where I worked through each Scrum role (Scrum Master, Product Owner, Developer, and Tester) over a single sprint.

## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating "user stories" help with this?

For me it starts with listening to what the client actually wants and not what I think they want. In the SNHU Travel project, the client meeting gave us a lot of info, but it wasn't until I wrote it out as user stories that it really made sense. Stories like Vacation Type Preference, Price Limit Filter, and Adjustable Destination List Size forced me to think about who the user was and why they needed the feature. Writing them that way also made it easier to set priorities and turn them into test cases later. Once the acceptance criteria were written down, I knew exactly what "done" looked like instead of guessing.

## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I like to break things into small pieces and get something working before worrying about making it perfect. The biggest lesson from this course was when the Product Owner changed direction and the slideshow had to switch to detox and wellness destinations. With a waterfall approach that would have been a big setback, but with Scrum it was just another change we adjusted to. Going forward I want to keep using Daily Scrums to stay on top of blockers, a prioritized backlog so I always know what matters most, and retrospectives so I'm actually learning from each sprint instead of repeating the same mistakes.

## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member means communicating early, especially when something changes or I'm stuck. It also means being willing to help outside of my own role. Going through every role in this project showed me how much each person depends on the others. The tester needs clear stories from the Product Owner, the developer needs good test cases, and the Scrum Master needs everyone to be honest about what's slowing them down. Working retail for 17 years taught me a lot of this already. A team only works when people show up, own their part, and look out for each other.
